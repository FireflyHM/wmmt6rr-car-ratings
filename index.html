How the ratings work

  • Acceleration comes from value A, which scales the drive force.
  • Top end averages value B (speed scale; higher top speed) and value C turned around (C is drag that grows with speed, so less is better).
  • Cornering comes from value D, which scales the tyres' sideways grip.
  • Each is scaled 0 to 10 across all cars. Overall is their average.
  • Top speed is the meter reading at a 17/17 full tune with a manual gearbox. Every car shares the same top gear, final drive, 300 mm wheels and 10,000 rpm limiter, so all cars top out at about 351-353 km/h; only value B moves it. An automatic shows about 1 km/h less. This matches the 351-352 km/h players see.
  • Grades are by rank: S top 10%, A next 20%, B middle 40%, C next 20%, D bottom 10%.
  • Each step of A or D changes force or grip by under 1%. A fully tuned car's power and grip come mostly from the tune, so these grades describe a car's character more than its pace.
  • Read from the game's code, not timed in races. A, B and D are traced to the physics; C and the weight offset are probable. Torque curve, drive layout, size and weight are shown but not scored.
